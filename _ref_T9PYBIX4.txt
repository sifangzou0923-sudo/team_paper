Performance Analysis and Prediction of Rock 
Cutting with a Rotating Abrasive Water Jet
Hongxiang Jiang1,2, Huihe Zhao1*   , Xiaodi Zhang1, Zijian Wu1 and Mingjin Zhao1 
Abstract 
During the excavation process of deep hard rock tunnels, precutting rock with an abrasive water jet can weaken 
their strength and improve the efficiency of mining machinery. However, owing to the complex geological environ-
ment, abrasive jets cannot fully utilize their rock-cutting performance. To fully exploit the advantages of high-pressure 
abrasive water jets, five orthogonal experiments were designed for rocks with significant differences in strength. 
Experimental research has been conducted on the performance of rotating abrasive waterjet-cutting rocks. Moreo-
ver, a neural network prediction model for predicting rock-cutting characteristics is established by comprehensively 
considering rock mechanics parameters and abrasive water jet parameters. The results show that the cutting depth 
of rocks with different strengths increases nonlinearly with increasing work pressure of the abrasive water jet. The 
cutting depth decreases exponentially with increasing cutting velocity. The cutting depth first increases and then 
decreases with increasing target distance, and the best target distance is between 4 mm and 6 mm. The effect 
of the target distance on the cutting width of rock is the most significant, but the cutting width of high-strength 
rock is not sensitive to changes in the working parameters of the abrasive water jet. The average relative errors of BP 
(backpropagation) neural networks optimized by global optimization algorithms in predicting rock cutting depth 
and width are 13.3% and 5.4%, respectively. This research combines the working characteristics of mining machinery 
to study the performance of abrasive waterjet rotary cutting of rocks and constructs a predictive model for the perfor-
mance of abrasive waterjet cutting of rocks that includes rock strength factors. This provides a new solution for quickly 
adjusting the working parameters of abrasive water jets according to mining conditions.
Keywords  Abrasive water jet, Cutting rock, Performance, Prediction
1  Introduction
In the process of deep hard rock roadway excavation, 
water jet precutting can reduce the difficulty of hard 
rock breaking under a state of stress [1, 2]. In addition, 
rock breaking assisted by water jets can reduce the load, 
degree of wear, and surface temperature of mechanical 
tools and extend their service life [3–6]. These findings 
indicate that water jet impact rock-breaking technol-
ogy has good application prospects in the field of deep 
underground energy extraction. However, the dynamic 
rotational rock-breaking performance of abrasive jets 
and the rapid adjustment of working parameters when 
encountering different types of underground rock layers 
have not been thoroughly investigated. In particular, in 
abrasive jet-assisted deep well petroleum drilling engi-
neering, rock properties gradually change with increas-
ing drilling depth [7]. At this point, adjusting the optimal 
working parameters of the abrasive jet promptly without 
stopping the equipment is necessary to achieve higher 
drilling efficiency. Therefore, many scholars have inves-
tigated the rock-breaking performance of abrasive water 
jets. Kaya et  al. [8] studied the influence of the basic

properties of abrasive particles and rocks on the cutting

cutting performance and the rock attribute parameters 
with a greater impact on the rock cutting depth. Oh and 
Cho [9] suggested that the maximum cutting depth of 
rocks with different hardnesses corresponds to the opti-
mal mass concentration and target distance of the abra-
sive water jet. Liu et al. [10] studied the effect of abrasive 
water jet concentration on the crushing performance of 
concrete and reported that the optimal concentration 
for abrasive water jet impact crushing of concrete is 20%. 
However, higher abrasive concentrations can acceler-
ate nozzle wear. To improve the rock-breaking ability of 
abrasive jets, many scholars have conducted research 
from the perspective of jet dynamics. Wang et  al. [11] 
verified that a slurry jet has a sufficiently high sand-carry-
ing capacity by comparing the erosion effects of a slurry 
jet and an abrasive jet. Mohammad Zadeh et al. [12, 13] 
studied the effect of fluid viscosity on particle transport 
and reported that high-viscosity and high-yield stress liq-
uids have a relatively high transport capacity. From this 
perspective, the fluid viscosity of an abrasive water jet 
can be changed by adding additives during the process 
of the abrasive water jet (AWJ) impacting rock breaking, 
thereby improving the ability of the abrasive particles to 
impact rock breaking.
Although increasing fluid viscosity improves the sand-
carrying capacity of abrasive jets, the flow characteristics 
of liquids with higher viscosity are influenced by other 
factors. For example, Akbari et  al. [14, 15] studied the 
influence of temperature and pressure on the particle 
transport performance of fluids with different viscosities 
via the Herschel Bulkley model. The results revealed that 
high temperatures and low pressures were not condu-
cive to the transport of rock debris. Increasing fluid vis-
cosity is a means of improving abrasive jet impact rock 
breaking. Many factors affect the impact of abrasive jets 
on rock breaking. Tan et al. [16] introduced the research 
progress of abrasive jets from the aspects of multiphase 
fluid dynamics, the material removal mechanism, and 
enhancement methods of the auxiliary abrasive jet cut-
ting effect. They noted that changing the type and size 
of abrasive particles can improve the processing effect of 
this technology by an order of magnitude. Hassan Zadeh 
et al. [17] studied the effects of different parameters on 
water jet behavior and reported that the yield stress of 
the environmental fluid hinders the development of the 
jet. Wu et  al. [18] studied the relevant factors affect-
ing the mixing performance of multiphase flow via the 
CFD‒DEM numerical simulation method. To provide 
a numerical simulation method for studying the mix-
ing effect of a premixed abrasive jet and improving the 
rock-breaking effect of jet impact on rocks. Ren et al. [19] 
studied the effects of the water jet impact velocity, inci-
dent angle, abrasive particle diameter, and concentration 
on the depth of rock fragmentation via numerical simu-
lations and experimental comparison methods. Ozcelik 
et al. [20] optimized the working parameters of a water 
jet to cut depth and width and obtained a cutability chart 
of a water jet cutting basalt. Liu et al. [21] reported that 
the rock-breaking ability of a water jet was the best when 
the incident angle was approximately 10°, and the rock-
breaking efficiency increased in a stepwise manner with 
increasing nozzle diameter. Li et  al. [22] demonstrated 
that there was an optimal target distance for water jet 
impact rock breaking and reported that the optimal 
target distance was approximately 10 times the nozzle 
diameter. Li et  al. [23] studied the process of abrasive 
jet impact rock breaking via the SPH–FEM method and 
concluded that the rock-breaking efficiency was highest 
when the inclination angle of the particle jet was within 
15°–20°. Wang et  al. [24] used the coupling method of 
SPH-FEM to study the optimal parameter setting of 
water jet-assisted TBM rock breaking. One of the conclu-
sions is that the optimal nozzle diameter for a water jet is 
1.5 mm.
The above research shows that there is an optimal 
working parameter for rock breaking by water jet impact, 
and its value is closely related to the strength of the rock. 
However, the energy consumption of abrasive water jets 
in rock breaking is high, which limits their use in the 
field of rock breaking to a certain extent [25]. Quickly 
adjusting the working parameters of the abrasive water 
jet according to geological conditions can significantly 
reduce the rock-breaking energy consumption of the 
abrasive water jet combined with machinery and greatly 
improve the rock-breaking efficiency. However, the rela-
tionship between the breaking characteristics of rock 
(heterogeneous, anisotropic) and the working param-
eters of abrasive water jet presented complex nonlinear 
changes. It is difficult to accurately describe the relation-
ship between the two by empirical. To solve this problem, 
many scholars have researched the matching relationship 
between jet parameters and rock-breaking characteris-
tics using the methods of multiple linear and nonlinear 
regression [26–30]. However, this method can only estab-
lish corresponding prediction models for specific types 
of rocks, and its generalization ability is poor. Therefore, 
it is necessary to study the change rule of the breaking 
index of rocks with different strengths with the working 
parameters of the abrasive water jet. The neural network 
prediction models have higher accuracy in predicting 
abrasive jet characteristics, and they can be used to pre-
dict various types of rocks [31–35]. The above research 
indicates that neural network has strong nonlinear fitting 
ability, and the BP neural network was applied earlier and

more widely. Many scholars use BP neural network mod-
els to predict the cutting performance of abrasive water 
jets [36–38]. Although this method solves the problems 
of small prediction range and poor accuracy in abrasive 
jet cutting performance, it still has certain limitations 
(such as local optimization of BP neural network, sensi-
tivity to initial weights, and low training efficiency).
Therefore, many optimization algorithms have been 
introduced to perfect the BP algorithm. Chen et al. [39] 
used the mind-evolved advanced genetic algorithm 
(MAGA) to improve the BP (backpropagation) neural 
network and built a prediction model of water jet erosion 
performance based on orthogonal experiment data. Deng 
et al. [40] established a prediction model of abrasive water 
jet cutting depth based on a BP neural network opti-
mized by GA (genetic algorithm) and established a visual 
module for predicting abrasive water jet cutting depth 
through programming. Jiang et al. [41] established a BP 
neural network prediction model optimized by adaptive 
mutation particle swarm optimization (AMPSO) based 
on experimental data of abrasive water jet cutting steel 
plates. Yin et al. [42] constructed a neural network model 
for sparrow search algorithm (SSA)-GA-BP based on 58 
sets of experimental data, which showed small errors in 
predicting the stability of the actual coal mine roof. Chen 
et al. [43] used a GA-BP neural network to optimize the 
structural parameters of the water jet nozzle and improve 
its coal breaking and punching performance. Zhang [44] 
established a rock burst level prediction model using a 
hybrid particle swarm optimization (PSO)-BP algorithm, 
and practical applications have proven that this method 
can effectively predict the rock burst level of tunnels. Gu 
et al. [45] compared and analyzed the performance of BP 
neural networks optimized by GA, PSO, and normaliza-
tion algorithms in reverse calculation of rock stress, and 
obtained that the PSO-BP model had the fastest conver-
gence speed and more stable results. Yan et al. [46] used 
PSO to optimize the initial parameters of the BP neu-
ral network, improving the stability and accuracy of its 
inversion of stress results.
According to this analysis, existing research on abrasive 
jets impacting rock breaking has revealed that most rock 
breaks in the form of fixed-point impact or parallel move-
ment. However, geo-energy mining machinery (such as 
pickaxe teeth, cutters, petroleum drilling machinery, and 
shield machines) mostly breaks rock via rotation. Moreo-
ver, many scholars only study specific types of rocks, and 
the generalizability of their conclusions is weak. From 
this, it can be concluded that there is still a certain gap in 
the study of the performance of abrasive water jet rotary 
rock cutting and the rapid adjustment of its working 
parameters under complex geological conditions. There-
fore, 5 types of rock (rocks with different hardnesses 
corresponding to different geological conditions at differ-
ent depths) were selected for the rotating abrasive water 
jet cutting experiment. On this basis, the performance of 
rotary abrasive jets in the cutting of rocks with different 
hardnesses was analyzed to construct a predictive model 
for rock during abrasive jet cutting, providing a solution 
for quickly adjusting working parameters in engineering 
applications.
Overall, based on a comprehensive literature analy-
sis, Section 1 elaborates on the lack of relevant research 
on the dynamic rotation of abrasive water jets for rock 
breaking in the existing literature. Combining with the 
phenomenon of variable rock layers in actual deep geo-
energy mining processes, a neural network prediction 
model is proposed to quickly adjust the working param-
eters of an abrasive water jet. Section 2 describes in detail 
the principle, test device, and process of genetic algo-
rithm and particle swarm optimization BP neural net-
work, as well as the method of obtaining cutting data. 
Section 3 analyzes the rock properties of abrasive water 
jet rotating grooving. Section 4 compares GA-BP, PSO-
BP, and BP neural networks (not optimized). Finally, the 
conclusions are drawn in Section 5.
2  Methods
2.1  Acquisition and Processing of Experimental Data
2.1.1  Experimental Methods
To study the performance of premixed abrasive waterjet 
rotating slotted rock, neural network training samples 
are obtained. In this study, a premixed abrasive water jet 
rotating rock cutting experimental platform was built 
based on the existing experimental equipment. As shown 
in Figure 1(a), the speed of the speed-regulating motor 
and the direction of rotation were adjusted by the digital 
display controller (400 W) to realize the premixed abra-
sive water jet to rotate the slotted rock. The fixing bolt 
was adjusted to change the initial target distance of the 
abrasive water jet. The working pressure of the system 
was controlled by the regulating valve. The nozzle diam-
eter used in the experiment was 1 mm, and the rock size 
was 300 mm×300 mm×100 mm. The pressure regulat-
ing range of the high-pressure pump was 0–40 MPa, the 
rotation speed adjustment range was 0.5–2.5 r/min, the 
rotation radius was 346 mm, the target distance adjust-
ment range was 2–10 mm, and the abrasive was 80 mesh 
garnet. The cutting width on the rock surface was nar-
row, and the groove formed by the premixed abrasive 
water jet rotation cutting the rock was mostly V shaped, 
so it was impossible to accurately measure the cutting 
depth with a depth gauge. As shown in Figure 1(b), in this 
study, a probe (very thin) was used to measure the rock-
cutting depth indirectly, and a Vernier caliper was used 
to measure the rock-cutting width directly. Owing to the

inconvenience of using the upper part of a Vernier caliper 
to measure the width of rock-cutting grooves and seams, 
measurement errors can easily occur. The surface of the 
rock is relatively flat, making it more convenient and 
accurate to measure the lower part with a Vernier cali-
per. Therefore, in this study, the groove width was meas-
ured after premixed abrasive water jet cutting of rocks 
through the lower half of the Vernier card.
The specific experimental process was as follows. (1) 
The sand supply valve of the abrasive tank was closed, 
and 80 mesh quartz sand was added to it. (2) The initial 
target distance was adjusted, and the sand supply valve of 
the high-pressure pump and abrasive tank was opened. 
(3) Adjust the cutting position under low pressure, the 
premixed abrasive water jet was taken continuously at 
the nozzle outlet, and its concentration was measured. 
(4) The pressure of the pump station was increases, and 
the digital display controller was adjusted to rotate the 
cutting rock.
2.1.2  Collection of Material Parameters
The rock hardness almost always affects the excava-
tion speed of rock tunnels in coal mines and the drilling 
efficiency of oil wells. Under complex geological condi-
tions, rock hardness may significantly differ at the work-
ing face of rock tunnels. However, the optimal working 
parameters of the abrasive jet are not the same when 
rock samples with different hardnesses are cut. A predic-
tion model with stronger applicability for rock hardness 
can help quickly adjust the working parameters of abra-
sive jets under different working conditions. Therefore, 
in this work, several kinds of rock, which are commonly 
encountered in tunnel excavation, are selected as samples 
for the abrasive jet-cutting experiment. Granite, blue-
stone, limestone, and sandstone can facilitate procure-
ment in the stone market, whereas concrete is made by 
us to increase the range of rock hardness and supplement 
shallow geological conditions. There are two purposes for 
conducting premixed abrasive water jet rotary cutting 
experiments on various rocks in this study. (1) Exploring 
the variation law of the cutting characteristics of differ-
ent rock types as a function of premixed abrasive water 
jet working parameters. (2) Providing training data for 
constructing a rock-cutting prediction model that adapts 
to complex geological conditions. The uniaxial compres-
sive strength and tensile strength of rock are important 
parameters for characterizing rock hardness, which 
affects the difficulty of abrasives cutting rock. Therefore, 
the mechanical performance parameters of rocks need 
to be measured and recorded. The hardness of rocks var-
ies under different geological conditions or geological 
depths, which is manifested mainly in inconsistent uni-
axial compressive strength and tensile strength. There-
fore, this study selects five types of rock with significant 
differences in hardness for cutting tests based on the rock 
types commonly encountered in practical engineering. 
Figure 2 shows the results of uniaxial and Brazilian split-
ting tests for five different rock types.
The specific values of the uniaxial compressive strength 
and tensile strength of the test samples are shown in 
Table  1. In engineering, rocks with a Vickers hardness 
of f < 8 are usually classified as soft rocks, whereas rocks 
with a Vickers hardness of f > 12 are classified as hard 
rocks. This is not entirely the same as the law of rock’s 
cutting characteristics of different hardnesses with the 
working parameters of the abrasive jet. When data on 
abrasive jet rotation rock cutting are processed, rock 
hardness has an impact on the characteristics of rock 
cutting, especially when the rock hardness f exceeds 10. 
Moreover, there is a significant difference in the depth 
and width of the cutting seam between harder and softer 
rocks. Taken together, the curve of their variation with

2.1.3  Measuring Cutting Parameters
The width and depth of rock grooves are important indi-
cators that affect rock fragmentation. To obtain more 
comprehensive experimental data, four locations of rock 
cuttings were measured in this study. As shown in Fig-
ure 3, the cutting positions at angles of 90°, 85°, 80° and 
75° between the premixed abrasive water jet and the 
upper surface of the rock were taken for measurement. 
To ensure the accuracy of the measurement data, we used 
a probe with a tip thickness of less than 0.02 mm to assist 
a Vernier caliper in measuring the depth of rock cutting. 
First, we inserted the probe into the rock gap, marked it 
with a marker pen, and measured the depth of the probe 
into the rock gap through the Vernier caliper, indirectly 
measuring the depth of rock cutting. To avoid acciden-
tal operational errors, we repeat the measurements three 
times at each measurement position, the average value 
for recording, and the average value of the measurements 
on both sides were taken for the symmetrical position.
2.2  BP Neural Network and Optimization Algorithm
A neural network prediction model based on obtaining a 
large amount of experimental data. As shown in Figure 4, 
the BP neural network includes an input layer, a hidden 
layer, and an output layer. The loss function L was con-
structed by accumulating the square of the difference 
between the predicted value and the expected output. 
According to the partial derivative of the loss function 
to the weight and threshold, the prediction problem was 
transformed into the problem of seeking the extreme 
value of the loss function. By using the gradient descent 
method to update the descent direction and speed in 
real time, the minimum value of the loss function can be 
quickly achieved.

3  Analysis of Factors Affecting Cutting
In this study, the characteristics of rock slots at the ver-
tical angle of impact of the premixed abrasive water jet 
on the rock were selected, and its variation with respect 
to the parameters of the premixed abrasive water jet 
was analyzed. Using range analysis and variance analy-
sis, the best sequence and combination of premixed 
abrasive water jet rock cutting were determined. There 
is an inherent relationship between the working pres-
sure, target distance, and lateral velocity of the abrasive 
jet, but their correlation is relatively small. In contrast, 
individual changes in different parameters have a more 
significant effect on the rock fragmentation effect. 
The impact of the working pressure of the premixed 
abrasive water jet on the rock fragmentation effect is 
reflected in the maximum impact velocity, the impact 
of the linear velocity on the rock is reflected mainly in 
the impact time, and the impact of the target distance 
on the rock is related mainly to the nozzle structure 
and reflux collision. Moreover, analyzing the correla-
tion between various parameters of the abrasive jet and 
the impact of rock fragmentation cannot yield valuable 
results. Therefore, this study analyzes the impact of 
each parameter alone on the effect of rock cutting.

3.1  Depth
Table  2 shows the influences of pressure, target dis-
tance, and linear velocity (speed × radius of rotation) of 
a premixed abrasive water jet on granite cutting depth 
according to range analysis. (The primary and secondary 
influences on the granite cutting depth were the working 
pressure, target distance, and linear velocity). The greater 
the range value is, the greater the influence of this param-
eter on the rock-cutting depth. According to Table 2, the 
combination of parameters with a premixed abrasive 
water jet working pressure of 35 MPa, a target distance 
of 4 mm, and a linear velocity of 1.08 m/min within the 
experimental range can maximize the granite cutting 
depth.
As shown in Figure  6, the influence trend of differ-
ent factors on the cutting depth was plotted accord-
ing to the arithmetic mean value k of the test results. 
The cutting depth of granite generally increases with 
increasing working pressure, increases first and then 
decreases with increasing target distance, peaking when 
the target distance is 4 mm. Notably, the granite cutting 
depth increases with increasing target distance from 
8 mm–10 mm. However, when the target distance con-
tinues to increase, the premixed abrasive water jet loses 
its rock-breaking ability, and the granite surface no longer 
forms obvious cuttings. Since the rock-breaking ability 
of the premixed abrasive water jet with a target distance 
of more than 10 mm within the scope of the test equip-
ment was lost and exceeded the scope of the experimen-
tal research, this study does not show the changing trend 
of the rock-cutting depth beyond the target distance of 
10 mm. The lower the linear velocity was, the longer the 
continuous impact time of the premixed abrasive water 
jet on the rock was, and the larger the characteristic 
index value of the rock cutting depth should theoretically 
be. This may be due to the unstable mass concentration 
of premixed abrasive water jet during the test, as the rock 
cutting depth in the range of 4.25–5.28 m/min increased 
with increasing linear velocity, but the rock cutting depth 
decreased with increasing linear velocity, which was still 
consistent with the theoretical analysis results.
The higher the outlet pressure of the water pump is, 
the higher the velocity of the premixed abrasive water jet 
at the nozzle outlet is, and the greater the impact force 
when it contacts the rock, making it easier for the rock 
to break and form a groove. After being ejected from the 
nozzle, the premixed abrasive water jet is affected by the 
air resistance, and its impact velocity continuously decays 
and diverges roughly into a conical shape. The velocity 
attenuation of water and abrasive particles located at the 
edge of the jet is more severe. Although the area of the 
premixed abrasive water jet hitting the rock increases 
with increasing target distance, it cannot cause greater 
damage to the rock because of the decrease in impact 
velocity. The linear velocity of a premixed abrasive water 
jet that cuts rock affects mainly the time to strike the 
rock. After the initial groove morphology is formed, the 
rock further fractures and deepens the groove depth 
under the dynamic load formed by the continuous 
impact of the abrasive water jet. However, as the depth of 
the groove increases, the distance of the premixed abra-
sive water jet hitting the rock also does not increase, and 
the motion of the residual jet in the narrow slit affects the 
speed of the abrasive jet hitting the rock. Therefore, as 
the linear velocity increases, the depth of the rock groove 
continuously becomes shallower.
To verify the accuracy of the factor combination 
obtained from the range analysis, this study conducted 
variance analysis on the granite orthogonal test data. The 
significance of each factor was obtained through the F 
test. The smaller the sig value is, the greater the influence 
of the corresponding factor on the rock-cutting charac-
teristic index. From Table 3, the order of influence on the 
granite cutting depth was still pressure, target distance, 
and linear velocity, which was consistent with the range 
analysis.

3.2  Width
In practical applications, the impact of rock fragmenta-
tion due to multiple cutting widths is much smaller than 
the impact of the cutting depth. When the optimal work-
ing parameters of premixed abrasive water jet rotary cut-
ting were selected, the main criterion was the depth of 
the cutting. Therefore, this study does not prioritize the 
working parameters of abrasive water jets based on the 
cutting width but rather analyzes the changing trend of 
the cutting width of five materials with abrasive water 
jets.
As shown in Figure  11, the width of the granite cut-
tings tends to expand with increasing working pressure 
of the abrasive water jet, and the peak value of the rock 
cutting width occurs at a working pressure of 28  MPa. 
The cutting width decreases as the jet pressure continues 
to increase, but the cutting width remains above 1.3 mm. 
Affected by the air resistance, the premixed abrasive 
water jet diffused with increasing distance, so the cut-
ting width increased with increasing target distance. 
The backflow of the premixed abrasive water jet impact-
ing rock scours the formed rock and further expands the 
rock cutting width. The contribution of the linear veloc-
ity to the cutting width was reflected mainly in the effect 
of backflow scouring, but the hard rock was not sensitive 
to the time of backflow scouring, so the change in lin-
ear velocity had no obvious impact on the cutting width 
of the granite. At the initial stage of working pressure 
increase, the cutting depth was shallow, and the impact 
damage of the abrasive particles and water and backflow 
scouring increased the cutting width. However, with 
increasing pressure, the contact surface moves down-
ward, and the increasing impact kinetic energy is used to 
increase the cutting depth. The water layer formed in the 
cutting had a certain buffering effect on the jet, which to 
some extent weakened the backflow scouring ability of 
the jet. Therefore, the rock cutting width decreases with 
increasing pressure above 28 MPa.
As shown in Figure 12, the cutting width of the blue-
stone always increased with increasing pressure. The 
increase in pressure caused the impact dynamic pressure 
of the premixed abrasive water jet rise, resulting in an 
increase in the stagnation pressure during cutting. Until 
the pressure reaches the secondary crushing condition 
of bluestone, this may be the main reason that the cut-
ting width continues to increase with pressure. The width 
of the bluestone cuttings increased with increasing tar-
get distance, which conforms to the distribution law of 
the water jet velocity field in the air domain. The cutting 
width of the bluestone changed slightly with linear veloc-
ity, which once again confirms that the cutting width of 
the hard rock was less affected by backflow scouring.
Compared with that of hard rock, the cutting width of 
low-strength materials was significantly affected by the 
working parameters of the premixed abrasive water jet 
compared with hard rock. In this work, limestone, sand-
stone, and concrete were analyzed together. As shown 
in Figure 13, there was no obvious change in the ampli-
tude of limestone cutting width with increasing pres-
sure, and the cutting width was smaller than those of 
sandstone and concrete. The amplitude of the cutting 
width of the sandstone and concrete was maintained at 
a high level, especially when the amplitude of the cutting 
width of the concrete changed greatly. There is a signifi-
cant "water hammer pressure" at the impact point when 
the premixed abrasive water jet impacts the rock, caus-
ing rapid surface damage and the formation of grooves. 
After the premixed abrasive water jet cuts the rock, the 
width of the cut basically changes. The impact point of 
the premixed abrasive water jet continues to move down-
ward and is no longer in contact with the edge of the rock 
groove, gradually weakening the influence of the pump 
pressure on the width of the rock groove. The impact of 
changes in pump pressure on the depth of rock grooves 
mainly includes the degree of damage caused by the 
initial impact of the jet and the continuous erosion of 
residual abrasive water jets. When the cutting depth was 
shallow, the premixed abrasive water jet diffused along 
the cutting area toward the free surface and continuously 
impacted the edge of the cutting area, resulting in wider

4  Prediction of Cutting Characteristics
4.1  Network Construction and Data Preprocessing
Figure 17 shows the single hidden layer BP neural network 
structure determined according to the basic mechanical 
parameters of the rock and the working parameters of the 
abrasive water jet. Its input neurons include six parameters 
( x1 −x6 ): compressive strength, tensile strength, pressure, 
target distance, linear velocity, and angle of incidence. The 
depth and width of the slit are the output neurons (y1 −y2) . 
The weights and thresholds between the layers of the neu-
ral network are shown. The hardness of rock in engineering 
determines the excavation speed of rock tunnels, and the 
uniaxial compressive strength and tensile strength are the 
main parameters that characterize rock hardness. There-
fore, among the numerous basic rock parameters, only 
uniaxial compressive and tensile parameters are used as 
output neurons of the network. The selection of abrasive jet 
parameters was based on the convenience of adjustment in 
practical applications. The parameters (diameter, concen-
tration, and particle size) that are difficult to adjust quickly 
at the excavation site were not used for model training. The 
reasons are as follows. Changing the diameter requires the 
machine to be stopped to replace the nozzle. At present, 
the concentration values of premixed abrasive water jet 
generating equipment are mostly fixed. Adjusting the parti-
cle size requires not only equipment shutdown but also the 
removal of residual abrasives from the tank and high-pres-
sure pipe. The purpose of constructing a prediction model 
is to provide the optimal parameter selection scheme for 
premixed abrasive waterjet cutting of rocks based on a 
real-time excavation environment. The effect of premixed 
abrasive water jet cutting is fully utilized to reduce rock 
strength and avoid excessive energy consumption due 
to inappropriate jet parameter combinations. Therefore, 
the premixed abrasive water jet parameters selected dur-
ing the training of the neural network are easily adjust-
able. Many parameters affect the rock-cutting index. To 
improve mechanical rock-breaking devices in the future, 
we will continue to supplement experiments to build more 
parameter-controlled predictive models. The heterogeneity 
and anisotropy of rocks often lead to abnormal test results 
in abrasive water jet-cutting rock tests. Although repeated 
experiments were conducted on abnormal cuttings in the 
experiment, the possibility that there were inappropri-
ate statistical data cannot be avoided. For example, during 
the working process of a premixed abrasive water jet, the 
instability of mass concentration often occurs, especially in 
the starting stage of the system. This phenomenon caused 
abnormal cuttings in the test process. The existence of 
abnormal data points affects the training process of neural 
networks and the accuracy of prediction results. However,

5  Conclusions
This work analyzed the performance of premixed abra-
sive water jet cutting of hard and soft rock. A neural 
network prediction model of cutting characteristics, 
including rock strength parameters, was established. The 
prediction performances of the GA-BP, PSO-BP, and BP 
neural networks without optimization were compared 
and analyzed.
(1)	 The optimal order of factors and specific values 
of different materials used for abrasive water jet 
rotary cutting differ. In the range of the test, the 
cutting depth increases nonlinearly with increasing 
working pressure, decreases with increasing linear 
velocity, and increases first and then decreases with 
increasing target distance. The cutting depth of 
concrete always tends to decrease with increasing 
target distance.
(2)	 Rock strength is an important factor affecting the 
cutting performance of abrasive water jets, and it 
affects mainly the rate of change in groove charac-
teristics. The influence of pressure on the cutting 
width is reflected mainly in the stagnation pres-
sure, reflux velocity, and time during cutting, and 
the cutting width of soft rock is more sensitive to 
changes in working pressure. The cutting width of 
low-strength materials is strongly affected by back-
flow scouring at a small target distance, and the 
cutting width at a large target distance is affected 
mainly by the impact distance. In addition, the 
influence of the linear speed on the cutting width is 
not obvious.
(3)	 The optimized BP neural network has better predic-
tion performance. The average level of the GA-BP 
neural network prediction error for the rock cutting 
depth and width index is 13.3% and 5.4%, respec-
tively, whereas GA-BP is not better than PSO-BP 
every time.
